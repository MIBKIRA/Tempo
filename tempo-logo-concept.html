These are the resolved "Ti" state, static — the draw-and-release only plays as a hero moment on the sign-in screen, not everywhere the mark appears.

The one creative call I made without asking: a "T" and an "i" can't both sit fully spelled-out inside one small square icon without either looking cramped. So this isn't two letters side by side — it's one mark with two readings. Read the crossbar and stem together, it's "T". Read just the stem and the dot above it, it's "i". The animation is what teaches you the second reading exists: the string draws back, snaps, and leaves a spark behind at the top — that spark is the dot. Nothing about the "T" reading changes or disappears; the "i" is additive, not a replacement.

One deliberate break from the hard-edge system everywhere else: the crossbar's bend is the one curved, rounded joint in the whole mark. A drawn string doesn't kink at a sharp corner — it curves. Same principle as the Evening glyph being the one curved icon in that set: one soft exception, used on purpose, not everywhere.
